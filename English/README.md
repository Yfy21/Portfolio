# About this Portfolio

This portfolio is built on the following premisse: as knowledge workers, the intermediate products we create in order to complete larger projects are just as important as those projects themselves and should also be valued and exalted. Therefore, aside from full-fledged projects, which you'll encounter in any portfolio, here you'll also encounter useful code snippets for the daily lives of data analyts as well as reflections on their importance and how they work. If you're interested in vetting my hability to transform raw data in meaningful and actionable information, you may focus on the "Case Studies" directory. However, if you want to check for a deeper understanding of data science's daily problems, you should check the "Code Snippets" folder.

## Why Organize My Portfolio this Way?

The idea of basing my portfolio on such a premisse occured to me while reading Tiago Forte's *Building a Second Brain*, an excellent book in which the author advocates that we should invest our time in creating a cognitive system outside of our brains — an extended mind — and explains how we can achieve that. The whole system proposed in the book is really clever and highly efficient for helping us track and complete our projects — I definitely recommend the reading —, but the concept of "intermediate packets" stands out as a key with which we can change our perspective on work.

Frequently, Forte says, we view work as an effort to accomplish big projects — even though modern project management may break such tasks into smaller ones, the default attitude is to relate to work by the energy spent in accomplishing a big task like organizing an event, writing a report, or creating a website. Obviously, since we are approaching our daily activities from a negative perspective (of that which is lost in the process), the tendency is to resent work and to view tasks as something we need to get rid of as quickly as possible. However, when we face work through the lens of intermediate packets, we begin to approach it from the standpoint of **created value**.

What most of us may not realize in our daily lives is that our work crystallizes in its products. Therefore, as we split big projects into smaller tasks and complete them, we may indeed be spending energy, but we are also sedimenting our knowledge and skills into a variety of outputs: an event's agenda; a graph with statistics on customer retention; a piece of code that solves a database connectivity issue; etc. These are the intermediate packets — the building blocks necessary to complete the larger intellectual endeavors of which they are a part — and what is great about them is that they are **storable** and **reusable**: if we cannot take back the energy we spent in a project, at least we can keep the products we have created in the process and use them on future occasions.

Unlike projects, which are singular, context-specific endeavors due to the complex ways in which many factors interact, intermediate packets solve specific needs and can easily be repurposed due to their more restricted nature. Also, because they are defined, delimited products, they are easily shareable and allow for more constant (and sincere) feedback from stakeholders, allowing for improvements and alignments of expectations early in the process. They also facilitate collaborative work and can be built upon on future occasions, which improves their quality through the further crystallization of (collective) work and knowledge.

Thinking in terms of intermediate packets instead of tasks can change our whole approach to work, encouraging a long-term collective perspective that is bound to improve quality because it deals with skills and knowledge as cumulative, nonexclusive resources. That is why I chose to build my portfolio not only with full-fledged projects, but with code snippets that solve different daily needs of a data analyst as well. The idea is to take this as an opportunity for deepening my knowledge and developing a finer understanding of the available tools and how they apply to different business contexts. The aim is to build the portfolio progressively with code snippets that increase in complexity and usefulness and to reflect on them. For that, I will be numbering each new addition in the order it takes place so that anyone can follow the path in the logical order of updates.
